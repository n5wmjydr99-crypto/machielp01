## Q1. Which Spanish speech datasets (and dataset-derived voices) were recorded with consent and permissive licenses, and what do their terms allow?

### Takeaway
The clearest-licensed Latin American source is Google's six crowdsourced TTS corpora (CC BY-SA 4.0). They are 48 kHz recordings by volunteers, quality-checked by native speakers, but split across many speakers with only a few minutes each, and the share-alike clause carries legal uncertainty. The LibriVox-derived sets are permissive but have mostly mixed or unverified accents and audiobook-style audio of variable quality: MLS and CML-TTS are CC BY 4.0, M-AILABS is BSD-style with commercial use allowed, and CSS10 and the carlfm 120 h set are public domain or CC0. Common Voice is CC0 but forbids trying to identify speakers. Several official Spanish Piper voices (davefx, sharvard, ald) are fine-tuned from the research-only "Lessac" checkpoint, so they are not clearly cleared for commercial use. The provenance of es_MX-claude and es_AR-daniela is unverified.

### Cited Findings

**Google crowdsourced Latin American Spanish (OpenSLR SLR61, 71–75)**
- Chilean (SLR71) and Venezuelan (SLR75) are licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 (Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International). The Google copyright notice reads 2018, 2019. — [OpenSLR 71](https://openslr.org/71); [OpenSLR 75](https://www.openslr.org/75)
- Argentinian (SLR61) is also CC BY-SA 4.0. The page lists separate female and male index files and audio archives and describes recordings of native speakers made in Buenos Aires. — [OpenSLR 61](https://www.openslr.org/61/); [ELDA mirror of SLR61](https://openslr.elda.org/resources/61/about.html)
- Colombian (SLR72) is CC BY-SA 4.0 with female and male archives. The Colombian and Puerto Rican releases say the data was "manually quality checked, but there might still be errors". — [OpenSLR 72](https://www.openslr.org/72/); [OpenSLR 74](https://www.openslr.org/74/)
- The Peruvian (SLR73) excerpt only says "see the LICENSE file", so I did not confirm its license text, though all its sibling sets are CC BY-SA 4.0. — [us.openslr.org SLR73 about](https://us.openslr.org/resources/73/about.html)
- Puerto Rico (SLR74) appears to be **female-only**: only `es_pr_female.zip` is listed. It is described as "transcribed high-quality audio of Puerto Rico Spanish sentences recorded by volunteers". — [OpenSLR 74](https://www.openslr.org/74/); [HF openslr loader](https://huggingface.co/datasets/openslr/openslr)
- Paper: Guevara-Rukoz et al., "Crowdsourcing Latin American Spanish for Low-Resource Text-to-Speech", LREC 2020. It presents six open corpora (Argentinian, Chilean, Colombian, Peruvian, Puerto Rican, Venezuelan) from native speakers of each region (or the diaspora for PR and VE). Google describes them as among the first high-quality free multi-speaker datasets for these dialects, "unencumbered by a restrictive license". — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.801); [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/pub49150/)
- Recording setup: a laptop, a Neumann KM 184 microphone and an XLR-USB converter, recorded at 48 kHz mono. Trained native speakers reviewed the recordings, and takes with noticeable background noise were re-recorded. The script was first written for Mexican Spanish and then adapted for each dialect. — [LREC 2020 paper PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.801.pdf)
- Speaker counts reported in the paper: 31 Chilean, 33 Colombian, 38 Peruvian and 23 Venezuelan native speakers. A 2026 paper that uses the combined set reports 16-bit PCM at 48 kHz, 37 h in total (19 h female, 18 h male) and 176 unique speakers. — [LREC 2020 paper PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.801.pdf); [arXiv 2602.24002 (secondary)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.24002)
- A community attempt to train a Piper es_AR voice from SLR61 reported that "r/rr" sounds were handled poorly and that there were only about 140 samples per voice, likely too few. — [larcanio/piper-voices discussion (HF mirror)](https://worker-huggingface.ww6410.workers.dev/larcanio/piper-voices/discussions/2)
- A third-party Kaggle repack of these corpora downsampled the audio to 22,050 Hz for TTS. — [Kaggle slr-latam-es-voices](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/igortamara/slr-latam-es-voices)
- No search result described the consent procedure or the speakers' ages. The SLR74 page says only "volunteers". — [search summary of LREC paper / OpenSLR pages](https://aclanthology.org/2020.lrec-1.801.pdf)

**Mozilla Common Voice (Spanish)**
- License CC0. Download terms on Hugging Face gated cards (CV 11.0 and 16.0) and on Mozilla Data Collective listings require you to agree "not to attempt to determine the identity of speakers". The Spanish 23.0 listing on Mozilla Data Collective also says you will not re-host or re-share the dataset. — [HF Common Voice 16.0](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mozilla-foundation/common_voice_16_0); [HF CV 11.0 card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mozilla-foundation/common_voice_11_0/blame/main/README.md); [Mozilla Data Collective, CV Scripted Speech 23.0 Spanish](https://datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmflnuzw51ddgmwjkxpm9z1lw)
- When asked whether the clause rules out speaker-identification models, a Mozilla staffer deferred to the legal team, so the clause's exact scope is officially unclear. — [Mozilla Discourse](https://discourse.mozilla.org/t/speaker-ids-for-speaker-identification-model/114358)

**LibriVox-derived sets (public-domain audiobook recordings)**
- **CSS10 Spanish:** a single LibriVox reader, "Tux", with about 23 h 50 min drawn from three Pérez Galdós audiobooks (Bailén; El 19 de Marzo y el 2 de Mayo; La Batalla de los Arapiles). — [CSS10 GitHub](https://github.com/Kyubyong/CSS10)
- CSS10 Spanish license: a Hugging Face mirror lists CC0 1.0, while a dataset catalog says the license is "reported inconsistently across sources" and to verify before redistribution. — [HF mirror (perezGaldos)](https://huggingface.co/datasets/vpuente/perezGaldos); [VoxKitchen CSS10](https://voxkitchen.readthedocs.io/en/latest/datasets/css10/)
- **M-AILABS:** most of the data comes from LibriVox and Project Gutenberg. The license states: "Redistribution and use in any form, including any commercial use, with or without modification are permitted provided that the following conditions are met". The conditions are to keep the copyright notice ("Copyright (c) 2017-2019 by the original creators @ M-AILABS") and not to use the contributors' names to endorse derived products. — [M-AILABS license text (rhasspy/larynx)](https://github.com/rhasspy/larynx/blob/master/licenses/m-ailabs.txt); [UMBC catalog](https://cisaad.umbc.edu/the-m-ailabs-speech-dataset/)
- A Piper-voice repackager says the BSD-style M-AILABS license permits commercial use and redistribution if the notice is kept. A fork maintainer says the audio is public domain except the Ukrainian portion. — [cstr/piper-voices-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/cstr/piper-voices-GGUF); [i-celeste-aurora/m-ailabs-dataset](https://github.com/i-celeste-aurora/m-ailabs-dataset)
- **Multilingual LibriSpeech (MLS):** CC BY 4.0. The Spanish archive is `mls_spanish.tar.gz` (50 GB; 14 GB in Opus). Before filtering, LibriVox Spanish held 1,438.41 h, 285 books and 120 speakers (this is not the final MLS split). — [OpenSLR 94](https://www.openslr.org/94); [MLS paper (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2012.03411)
- **CML-TTS:** CC BY 4.0, built on MLS and adapted for TTS. It covers seven languages including Spanish at 24 kHz, 3,233.43 h and 613 speakers in total. I did not find the Spanish-only split. — [CML-TTS paper (arXiv 2306.10097)](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.10097v1); [HF card ylacombe/cml-tts](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ylacombe/cml-tts/blob/main/README.md)
- **carlfm01 "120h Spanish" set:** 120 h of LibriVox Spanish, automatically segmented and validated, listed as Public Domain. One mirror notes the audio is low-rate (16 kHz). — [Mozilla Discourse release post](https://discourse.mozilla.org/t/releasing-my-spanish-dataset-120h-of-public-domain-data/41303); [HF mirror](https://b-61j.pages.dev/datasets/Blakus/Spanish_spain_dataset_100h)
- CIEMPIESS "LibriVox Spanish" on Hugging Face is CC BY-SA 4.0. The LDC "LibriVox Spanish" (LDC2020S01) is under an LDC user agreement, so it is not free. — [HF ciempiess/librivox_spanish](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ciempiess/librivox_spanish/raw/main/README.md); [LDC2020S01](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2020S01)

**Official Spanish Piper voices and their training data**
- Commercial-use issue: the `en_US-lessac` voice was trained on the Blizzard Challenge 2013 corpus, whose weights are described as limited to non-commercial research, educational and demonstration use unless Lessac Technologies authorizes otherwise. — [cchiou-amba/Piper model card](https://huggingface.co/cchiou-amba/Piper)
- Loudness.ai's audit of official Piper voices excluded 102 of 177 files because their license, or the Lessac voice they were fine-tuned from, forbids commercial use. Its excluded table lists **es_ES-davefx-medium, es_ES-sharvard-medium and es_MX-ald-medium** as "built on the Lessac voice". It notes that "the legal position of such models is unsettled" and excluded them as a precaution. — [Loudness.ai Piper ranking](https://loudness.ai/research/piper-tts-voices-ranked/)
- A game-dev licensing guide likewise treats Lessac and its fine-tunes as not cleared to ship, even when the fine-tune's own data is CC0. An open piper1-gpl issue asks maintainers to clarify the same "CC0 dataset but Lessac initialization" case. — [respawnstack Piper](https://respawnstack.com/tools/piper); [OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl #314](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl/issues/314)
- Older maintainer guidance (2023): "The Piper project is intended for text to speech research, and does not impose any additional licenses on the checkpoints or voice models", meaning the dataset licenses govern. — [rhasspy/piper discussion #271](https://github.com/rhasspy/piper/discussions/271)
- **es_ES-davefx:** packagers list the data as CC0 from NabuCasa voice-datasets, and one package spec says MIT. A catalog issue says it was "fine-tuned from Lessac (non-commercial dataset)". — [Repology](https://repology.org/project/piper-voice-es-es-davefx/information); [OpenMandriva spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenMandrivaAssociation/piper-voice-es_ES-davefx/master/piper-voice-es_ES-davefx.spec); [sidevoice issue #30](https://github.com/sidevoice/sidevoice-engine/issues/30)
- **es_MX-ald:** the model card says "Finetuned from Spanish davefx voice (medium quality)", trained on a Mexican Spanish speech dataset. The card's license field is garbled, and packagers list "Unlicense". — [ald MODEL_CARD (older commit)](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/blob/a60bfb1358818a92675b3a2d9d48fb8ea47035c1/es/es_MX/ald/medium/MODEL_CARD); [sidevoice issue #30](https://github.com/sidevoice/sidevoice-engine/issues/30)
- **es_ES-sharvard:** two speakers. The model card links Edinburgh DataShare and gives the CC BY 3.0 license. The underlying Sharvard corpus has 700 phonemically balanced sentences recorded by one male and one female native **peninsular** (Spain) Spanish talker. — [sharvard MODEL_CARD](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/raw/23fc3f9dd3c46fa7f2235964caa7be4e9b2e2e6f/es/es_ES/sharvard/medium/MODEL_CARD); [mijuanlo/piper-voices](https://huggingface.co/mijuanlo/piper-voices); [LASLab paper page](https://laslab.org/journal_paper/the-sharvard-corpus-a-phonemically-balanced-spanish-sentence-resource-for-audiology); [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/3547446)
- **es_MX-claude (high):** contributed by HirCoir. In the contribution thread he says he previously used Tacotron "to clone voices" and asked whether the license should be apache-2.0. I found no description of the training data. A third-party repackager lists it as apache-2.0. Loudness.ai ranks Claude the best Mexican Spanish voice (score 85.0). Its table says es_MX has 2 voices with a median of 79.5, which seems to conflict with ald being excluded. — [rhasspy/piper-voices discussion #10](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/discussions/10); [HirCoir/piper-voices](https://huggingface.co/HirCoir/piper-voices/tree/main/claude); [PYTHAI/voaice](https://huggingface.co/PYTHAI/voaice); [Loudness.ai ranking](https://loudness.ai/research/piper-tts-voices-ranked/)
- **es_AR-daniela (high):** an entry exists in rhasspy/piper-voices, but I found no source naming its dataset. A community es_AR Piper voice (larcanio) was trained on SLR61, but I could not confirm that it is the same "daniela". — [rhasspy/piper-voices v1.0.0 tree](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/tree/v1.0.0); [igortamara demo app](https://huggingface.co/spaces/igortamara/sample-tts-piper/blob/main/app.py); [larcanio discussion](https://worker-huggingface.ww6410.workers.dev/larcanio/piper-voices/discussions/2)
- **es_ES-carlfm:** a catalog issue describes it as public-domain data trained from scratch (not confirmed by the model card). Loudness.ai's best Spain-Spanish voice is "MLS 9972" (score 75.8). Scores are automatic (UTMOS22 naturalness plus Whisper large-v3 clarity, with no human panel). — [sidevoice issue #30](https://github.com/sidevoice/sidevoice-engine/issues/30); [Loudness.ai ranked dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Loudness-ai/piper-voices-ranked)
- Piper voice packs bundle espeak-ng data, which is GPL-3.0. That is separate from the voice model licenses. — [louiecartoon voice-pack release](https://github.com/louiecartoon/app-releases/releases/tag/voices-piper-v1)
- Precedent for a voice donor releasing a clean voice: a German Thorsten Piper voice trained "from scratch" with no third-party checkpoint and released under CC0 1.0. I found no Spanish equivalent. — [Thorsten-Voice/Piper-Scratch](https://huggingface.co/Thorsten-Voice/Piper-Scratch)

### Inferences
- **Best accent match for "Nico" (young Latin American man):** the male archives of SLR61, SLR71, SLR72 and SLR75 (and probably SLR73) are the only clearly licensed Latin American Spanish male studio-grade data found. Per-speaker data is small: about 37 h across 176 speakers is roughly 12–13 minutes per speaker on average, and one trainer reports about 140 utterances per voice. That suits multi-speaker training or blending far better than building a dedicated single-speaker clone. Speaker ages are undocumented, so a "19-year-old" timbre cannot be selected from metadata.
- **CC BY-SA 4.0 obligations:** commercial use is allowed. Attribution is required (credit Google/OpenSLR, link the license), and share-alike applies to adapted material you distribute. Whether trained model weights, or audio generated from them, count as "adapted material" is unsettled, and I found no source resolving it. The prudent practice is to give attribution in the video description and to release any redistributed model or derived dataset under CC BY-SA.
- **LibriVox-derived sets** (MLS, CML-TTS, M-AILABS, CSS10, carlfm) are legally permissive. Their accents are mixed or unverified, many readers are likely from Spain, and the audio is audiobook-style narration of variable amateur quality, which suits a natural "Nico" voice less well than the Google sets.
- **Common Voice** is legally CC0, but cloning one contributor into a recurring character conflicts with the spirit of the "do not identify speakers" term. Its crowd-recorded audio is also generally less suitable for TTS (no source found on Spanish CV audio quality, so this is inference).
- **Piper Spanish voices for monetized use:** davefx, sharvard and ald carry the Lessac-initialization risk. Claude and daniela have unverified provenance, and claude's author mentions past voice cloning, which is an extra reason to verify. None can currently be called clearly cleared for commercial use. Per secondary sources, carlfm and the MLS voices look cleaner, but they are Spain-labeled and low-quality.

## Q2. Which models or methods create invented voices that belong to no real person, and how good are they in Spanish?

### Takeaway
As of Nov 2026 there are four main free, commercially licensed routes to an invented Spanish voice: (a) Kokoro (Apache 2.0) blending of its three Spanish voice tensors; (b) text-described "voice design" with Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign (Apache 2.0) or Parler-TTS Mini Multilingual v1.1 (Apache 2.0); (c) preset voices in on-device models such as Supertonic 2/3 (OpenRAIL-M weights); (d) zero-shot cloning (Chatterbox Multilingual, MIT; Qwen3-TTS Base) fed with a synthetic reference clip from (a)–(c). Only Kokoro, Piper and Supertonic are comfortably CPU-friendly. No independent Spanish listening tests were found for any of them. XTTS-v2, F5-TTS and Fish Speech/OpenAudio weights are non-commercial and should be avoided.

### Cited Findings

**Kokoro-82M (Apache 2.0)**
- Weights are Apache 2.0. The developer says training used only "permissive/non-copyrighted" audio: public domain, Apache/MIT-licensed audio, and synthetic audio from closed commercial TTS providers, with no synthetic audio from open models and no "custom voice clones". The v1.0 card also lists CC BY sources (Koniwa, SIWIS). v1.0 used a few hundred hours of audio. — [Kokoro-82M card (HF, PR mirror)](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M/blob/refs%2Fpr%2F44/README.md)
- The Spanish voices are `ef_dora` (female), `em_alex` and `em_santa` (male), with lang_code `e` and espeak-ng `es` G2P (requires the espeak-ng package). — [Kokoro VOICES.md](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M/blob/main/VOICES.md); [DeepWiki kokoro languages](https://deepwiki.com/hexgrad/kokoro/4-languages-and-voices)
- No quality grade is published for the Spanish voices ("Grade not listed"). Descriptions from a catalog: Dora "warm, clear, conversational", Alex "steady, natural, measured", Santa "deep, cheerful, expressive" (marketing-style, not measured). — [kokoroweb voices](https://kokoroweb.app/voices); [Sandbook voices](https://sandbook.app/voices)
- Voice blending uses weighted formulas such as `0.3*af_sarah + 0.7*am_adam`. Blending works best between voices of the same language, because the language code comes from the first voice's prefix. — [streaming-tts](https://github.com/nikkoxgonzales/streaming-tts); [Kokoro-TTS Home Assistant integration](https://github.com/beecho01/Kokoro-TTS)
- CPU speed: Picovoice's 2026 on-device benchmark measured Kokoro at about 3,658 ms first-chunk latency, versus about 1,720 ms for Piper and about 2,612 ms for Supertonic 2 (vendor-run, English-oriented). A community report measured Kokoro ONNX at about 5.5 s for 196 characters on CPU. — [Picovoice on-device TTS benchmark](https://picovoice.ai/blog/on-device-tts/); [TalkTerm discussion #576](https://github.com/combsmith/TalkTerm/discussions/576)

**Parler-TTS Mini Multilingual v1.1 (Apache 2.0)**
- Apache 2.0, with all datasets, preprocessing, training code and weights released publicly. It was fine-tuned on cleaned CML-TTS plus the non-English part of MLS (about 9,200 h), plus about 580 h of English LibriTTS-R. — [Parler multilingual v1.1 card](https://huggingface.co/parler-tts/parler-tts-mini-multilingual-v1.1)
- v1.1 was "trained with some consistent speaker names". The Spanish row of its speaker table includes names such as "Steven" and "Olivia" (the search excerpt was partial). The 34-name speaker library (Jon, Lea, Gary…) belongs to the English checkpoints, not the multilingual one. — [Parler multilingual v1.1 card](https://huggingface.co/parler-tts/parler-tts-mini-multilingual-v1.1)
- By default Parler generates random voice characteristics. A user thread is titled "Speaker inconsistency limits usefulness". — [parler_tts Space discussion #17](https://huggingface.co/spaces/parler-tts/parler_tts/discussions/17)
- The authors' self-reported Spanish WER is 0.70%, the lowest of their 8 languages. There are no independent MOS or listening tests. — [HF blog: multilingual Parler recipe](https://huggingface.co/blog/PHBJT/multi-parler-tts)

**Qwen3-TTS (Apache 2.0)**
- The repo and PyPI package are Apache-2.0. The 1.7B-VoiceDesign model creates voices from text descriptions; the 0.6B and 1.7B Base models clone from reference audio. The README includes a "Voice Design then Clone" example that builds a reusable clone prompt from a VoiceDesign reference. — [QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS); [qwen-tts on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/qwen-tts/)
- Spanish is supported, but there is "no native-Spanish preset", and no independent Spanish naturalness tests were found. A community project ships 14 curated Spanish clone and design voices (Spain, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Colombia) for Apple Silicon/MLX. — [sidevoice issue #102](https://github.com/sidevoice/sidevoice-engine/issues/102); [alblez/qwen3-tts-spanish-voices](https://github.com/alblez/qwen3-tts-spanish-voices)

**Supertonic 2 / 3 (on-device, ONNX)**
- Supertonic 2 supports English, Korean, Spanish, Portuguese and French and runs "up to 167× faster than real-time". The code is MIT and the weights are **OpenRAIL-M**. The CLI accepts `--lang es`. — [Supertone/supertonic-2 card](https://huggingface.co/Supertone/supertonic-2); [supertonic on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/supertonic/1.1.2/)
- OpenRAIL-M "is not a fully permissive license" because it carries use restrictions. Supertonic 3 reportedly expands coverage to 31 languages under the same licensing. — [voicechanger.live Supertonic](https://voicechanger.live/text-to-speech/models/supertonic); [ScriptByAI Supertonic v3](https://www.scriptbyai.com/supertonic-free-on-device-tts/)

**Zero-shot cloning or tone-color models usable with a synthetic reference**
- Chatterbox Multilingual supports 23 languages including Spanish. Model metadata lists the MIT license. Every output carries an imperceptible "Perth" neural watermark, detectable with the `perth` library. CPU "works but is slow". — [Chatterbox multilingual docs (third-party mirror)](https://www.mintlify.com/yocxy2/chatterboxyocxy/models/chatterbox-multilingual)
- OpenVoice V2 has been MIT since April 2024 and natively supports Spanish. It depends on MeloTTS, which supports Spanish among other languages. — [myshell-ai/OpenVoiceV2](https://huggingface.co/myshell-ai/OpenVoiceV2)

**Models to avoid for monetized use (non-commercial weights)**
- XTTS-v2 weights fall under the Coqui Public Model License, which "allows only non-commercial use of a machine learning model and its outputs". Coqui has shut down, so no commercial license can be bought. — [PromptQuorum XTTS license](https://www.promptquorum.com/power-local-llm/local-tts-voice-cloning-piper-coqui-xtts)
- The F5-TTS pretrained models are CC-BY-NC because of their Emilia training data, and the restriction carries over to fine-tunes (including Spanish fine-tunes). Fish Speech/OpenAudio weights are non-commercial or research-only unless you sign a separate deal. — [Cartesia license comparison](https://www.cartesia.ai/learn/open-source-tts); [Yaps.ai license check](https://www.yaps.ai/blog/best-local-tts-models)

### Inferences
- **Most practical invented-voice path on a 4-core CPU with no GPU:** Kokoro blends of em_alex and em_santa for Nico, or ef_dora pitched and blended for Kori. Each blend is a fixed tensor, so it is reproducible and belongs to no single real person. The training data is described as permissive, though it is partly synthetic from closed providers. The main risks are only three Spanish source voices (limited timbre range) and an unverified accent: the espeak `es` G2P may yield Castilian pronunciation, which needs a listening test for a Latin American character.
- **Highest-quality invented voices:** Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign (describe "joven latinoamericano de 19 años…"), then freeze one chosen take as the reference for the Base clone model. This is likely the most natural option, but 0.6–1.7B models on a 4-core CPU will probably be slow (not measured; see Gaps). A workable pattern is to design the reference once (even on a free cloud GPU), then reuse the frozen reference offline.
- **Parler multilingual:** describing a voice in text creates an invented voice, but each run differs unless the description, speaker name and seed are all fixed. It is a weaker consistency choice than a frozen reference clip.
- **Supertonic 2/3:** probably the fastest CPU option with Spanish. Its preset voices are fixed, but they are vendor voices whose provenance I did not research, and OpenRAIL-M use restrictions must be read before commercial use.
- **A synthetic reference clip** from Kokoro, Qwen3 VoiceDesign or Parler can feed Chatterbox or OpenVoice for a richer delivery while staying an "invented" voice, because the timbre traces back to a synthetic, not a real, speaker.

### Gaps
- No independent Spanish MOS or listening comparisons for Kokoro, Parler multilingual, Qwen3-TTS, Chatterbox Multilingual or Supertonic were found.
- No measured CPU (4-core) speeds were found for Qwen3-TTS 0.6B/1.7B, Parler Mini Multilingual or Chatterbox Multilingual in Spanish.
- Whether Kokoro's Spanish voices sound peninsular or Latin American (espeak `es` vs `es-419` behavior) was not confirmed. One vendor page claims Kokoro Spanish is "optimized for Latin American and European Spanish" ([fal.ai](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/kokoro/spanish/llms.txt)), which is not authoritative.
- Supertonic's preset-voice provenance and the exact OpenRAIL-M use restrictions were not researched. Whether the MeloTTS license is MIT was not confirmed in this session. Random-style sampling in StyleTTS2-type Spanish models (e.g., [BSC-LT styletts2-spanish-multispeaker](https://huggingface.co/BSC-LT/styletts2-spanish-multispeaker)) was not researched, including its license.

## Q3. How to keep a character's voice identical across many videos (presets, fixed reference and seed, saved embeddings, deterministic settings)?

### Takeaway
The most reliable approach is a fixed-voice model: a Piper/Supertonic voice file or a saved Kokoro voice tensor, with frozen model files and fixed synthesis parameters. Then the timbre cannot drift. With zero-shot models, persist the conditioning once: Chatterbox `conds.pt`, Qwen3 `voice_clone_prompt`, or one canonical reference clip plus its exact transcript. Fix the seed and use conservative sampling, and audit with speaker-embedding similarity. Text-description-only voices (Parler, VoiceDesign) are the least stable unless frozen into a reference clip.

### Cited Findings
- **Chatterbox:** run `prepare_conditionals` once on a reference WAV, save with `model.conds.save(...)` to a `.pt` file, and later restore with `Conditionals.load(...)` (optionally with `map_location`). Later sessions then skip reference processing. — [Chatterbox Conditionals API (third-party docs mirror)](https://www.mintlify.com/yocxy2/chatterboxyocxy/api/conditionals)
- In Chatterbox, the "exaggeration" value is baked into the cached conditionals, so the `.conds.pt` must be rebuilt if exaggeration changes. Temperature is the randomness control ("lower = more deterministic"). No official seed guidance was found. — [xil-pipeline chatterbox worker](https://xil-pipeline.readthedocs.io/en/latest/xil-pipeline/src/xil_pipeline/chatterbox_worker/); [Chatterbox Conditionals API](https://www.mintlify.com/yocxy2/chatterboxyocxy/api/conditionals)
- **Qwen3-TTS:** the official README advises building a clone prompt once with `create_voice_clone_prompt` and passing it later via `voice_clone_prompt`. The "Voice Design then Clone" flow builds a reusable prompt from a designed reference. — [QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS)
- A third-party guide recommends designing a reference clip with VoiceDesign and then cloning from it for all lines, so the VoiceDesign model does not produce slightly different voices on each call. This is not confirmed in the official docs. — [ocdevel Qwen3-TTS guide (2026)](https://ocdevel.com/blog/20260302-qwen-tts-voice-cloning)
- ComfyUI wrappers can save Qwen3 voice-clone prompts to disk and reload them across sessions. The Base model's default in-context mode needs reference audio plus its transcript. — [ComfyUI-Qwen3-TTS](https://github.com/DarioFT/ComfyUI-Qwen3-TTS); [TTS-Audio-Suite](https://github.com/diodiogod/TTS-Audio-Suite)
- **Fixed seeds per character:** TTS Audio Suite (a multi-engine ComfyUI pack covering Chatterbox, F5, Qwen3, IndexTTS-2 and others) supports per-segment tags like `[Alice|seed:42]` for "consistent character voices with fixed seeds". Its "Save Character Voice" node requires the reference audio and its exact transcript. — [TTS-Audio-Suite](https://github.com/diodiogod/TTS-Audio-Suite)
- **Parler:** voices are random by default. Consistency comes from reusing the identical description and/or a named speaker. — [parler_tts Space discussion #17](https://huggingface.co/spaces/parler-tts/parler_tts/discussions/17); [Parler multilingual v1.1 card](https://huggingface.co/parler-tts/parler-tts-mini-multilingual-v1.1)
- **Drift risk and measurement:** TTS systems can show "gradual timbre drift or sudden identity collapse" over dialogue. A 2026 dubbing benchmark scores consistency by clustering speaker embeddings per character. — [SceneTTS-Bench (arXiv 2609.26255)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26255)
- Reference-clip practice from video-voice tools: use one 5–15 s sample that clearly represents the character in every generation, and expect a similar character rather than a forensic match. — [ClipDance Seedance 2.0 guide](https://clipdance.ai/blog/seedance-2-consistent-voice)
- **Kokoro blends** are weighted combinations of voice tensors (e.g., `0.3*voiceA + 0.7*voiceB`), so a chosen blend can be stored and reused as-is. — [streaming-tts](https://github.com/nikkoxgonzales/streaming-tts)
- **Robot effect for "Kori" with a fixed, scriptable chain:** FFmpeg documents a "robotize" example, `afftfilt=real='hypot(re,im)*sin(0)':imag='hypot(re,im)*cos(0)':win_size=512:overlap=0.75`, which zeroes the phase. A user who tried `win_size=4` got an error, so 512 is the documented safe value. — [FFmpeg cvslog commit adding afftfilt examples](https://lists.mplayerhq.hu/pipermail/ffmpeg-cvslog/2019-October/119470.html); [FFmpeg afftfilt docs (mirror)](https://ayosec.github.io/ffmpeg-filters-docs/5.0/Filters/Audio/afftfilt.html); [ffmpeg-devel thread](https://ffmpeg.org/pipermail/ffmpeg-devel/2020-March/258104.html)

### Inferences
- **Reliability ranking (most to least):**
  1. **Fixed-voice, fixed-file models** (Piper voice file, Supertonic preset, saved Kokoro blend tensor). Identity is a stored artifact, so only prosody varies. Pin the model file by checksum and the library version, and fix speed and noise parameters (e.g., Piper `length_scale`, `noise_scale`, `noise_w`).
  2. **Persisted conditioning for zero-shot models** (Chatterbox `conds.pt`, Qwen3 `voice_clone_prompt`, one canonical reference WAV and transcript), with a fixed seed and low or moderate temperature and exaggeration. The timbre is stable, but autoregressive sampling can still drift, so audit it.
  3. **Description-only design** (Parler, VoiceDesign called fresh each time). This is the least stable. Always freeze one approved take into a reference clip instead.
- **Practical "character bible" per voice:** store the model name, version and checksum; the voice ID, blend weights or reference WAV with transcript; the seed; the sampling parameters; the exact FFmpeg post-chain (EQ, pitch, robotize mix, loudness target); and the output sample rate. Regenerate a fixed test sentence for each video and compare its speaker-embedding cosine similarity against the canonical sample, following SceneTTS-Bench's embedding-based idea.
- **For a "light" robotic Kori:** the documented robotize filter is strong, because it fully removes phase. Mixing it at a low ratio with the dry voice (e.g., FFmpeg `amix`/`amerge` with fixed weights) and adding a fixed pitch shift keeps it natural. These exact mixing parameters are my suggestion, not taken from a source. Saving the full filter string in a script guarantees an identical effect every time.
- Changing engine versions (e.g., a Kokoro or Chatterbox update) can subtly change timbre even with the same voice or seed, so vendoring the exact model files is part of consistency. This is an inference; no source tested it.

### Gaps
- No official statement was found on whether Kokoro and Piper inference is bit-exact deterministic across runs and machines, or on seed handling in Chatterbox Multilingual.
- No source quantified drift for Qwen3-TTS or Chatterbox when using saved prompts versus re-processing the reference.

## Q4. Ethics: is it acceptable to build a commercial character voice from a single dataset speaker's recordings? What do dataset authors, model providers and voice-rights bodies recommend?

### Takeaway
A permissive dataset license (CC BY/BY-SA/CC0/public domain) usually makes it legally possible, but voice-rights bodies and emerging law treat a recognizable individual voice as something requiring specific, active consent for each new use. Dataset volunteers consented to a research or TTS dataset release, not explicitly to becoming a recurring commercial character. Common Voice also forbids identifying speakers, and LibriVox readers have voiced objections. The ethically safer pattern is an invented voice (blend or design), or multi-speaker training that does not reproduce one person, with attribution and AI-voice disclosure.

### Cited Findings
- NAVA's #fAIrVoices campaign (May 2023) rests on **consent, control, compensation**. It denounces synthetic voice training and sourcing "without the explicit active consent" of the artists. Its guidance says that "any additional or further use that has not been described will require new consent". It also offers an AI/Synthetic Voice contract rider. — [NAVA press release](https://navavoices.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/PRESS-RELEASE-fAIr.pdf); [NAVA: reasonable description of intended uses](https://navavoices.org/reasonable-description-of-intended-uses/); [NAVA Synthetic/AI Voices](https://navavoices.org/?p=5852); [Pozotron summary of the fAIr Voices pledge](https://blog.pozotron.com/understanding-the-nava-fairvoice-pledge)
- **Mexico:** an April 2025 bill would require "prior, informed and written consent" to use AI to create, clone, simulate or modify the voice of a dubbing artist or performer. In Sept 2025 Indautor and the government's legal office were working on a reform treating the voice as biometric data. In 2026 the press reported that the Chamber of Deputies approved a provision in the new Ley Federal de Cine y Audiovisual against AI-generated dubbing, and in May 2026 that sanctions for non-consensual cloning reach up to 40,000 days of wages. The final text published in the official gazette (DOF) was not verified. — [Gaceta Parlamentaria, 24 Apr 2025](https://gaceta.diputados.gob.mx/Gaceta/66/2025/abr/20250424-II-4.html); [Expansión, Sep 2025](https://expansion.mx/tecnologia/2025/09/30/mexico-va-por-regular-uso-ia-en-doblaje); [Xataka México](https://www.xataka.com.mx/legislacion-y-derechos/mexico-quiere-que-voz-sea-dato-biometrico-preparan-primera-ley-clonacion-voces-ia); [FayerWayer, Feb 2026](https://www.fayerwayer.com/entretenimiento/2026/02/15/mexico-tendra-una-ley-para-proteger-a-actores-de-doblaje-ante-la-irrupcion-de-la-inteligencia-artificial/); [El Imparcial, Mar 2026](https://www.elimparcial.com/espectaculos/2026/03/26/mexico-prohibe-el-uso-de-ia-para-generar-doblaje-bajo-la-nueva-ley-federal-de-cine-y-audiovisual-que-reconoce-la-voz-humana-como-herramienta-artistica-irrepetible-y-establece-sanciones-contra-la-clonacion-sin-consentimiento-para-proteger-el-trabajo-de-los-actores-frente-al-streaming/); [El Imparcial, May 2026](https://www.elimparcial.com/mexico/2026/05/15/ya-es-oficial-la-nueva-ley-protege-a-locutores-y-actores-de-doblaje-contra-la-clonacion-digital-con-multas-de-hasta-40-mil-dias-de-salario-por-usar-inteligencia-artificial-para-suplantar-su-labor-profesional-sin-consentimiento/)
- **EU AI Act Art. 50 (in force since 2 Aug 2026):** providers of systems that generate audio must mark outputs in a machine-readable form. Systems already on the market before 2 Aug reportedly have until 2 Dec 2026. Deployers publishing deepfakes must disclose them, with lighter, adapted disclosure for evidently creative or satirical works. Fines reach up to €15M or 3% of turnover. These are law-firm and vendor summaries, not the official text. — [Travers Smith briefing](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/is-it-a-bot-eu-ai-act-transparency-rules-take-effect-2-august-2026/); [artificialintelligenceact.eu guide to Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/?p=6131); [Greenberg Traurig, Jun 2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/6/deepfakes-chatbots-ai-generated-text-european-commission-details-transparency-obligations-under-the-ai-act); [netzstrategen](https://ai.netzstrategen.com/en/insights/ai-content-disclosure-rules/)
- **LibriVox:** recordings are public domain, but users have raised concern about using voices "without expressed consent". One regular reader said this concern is a reason they record less. Another forum post raised likeness rights and livelihood risks. LibriVox describes itself as audiobooks "read in real time by real people". No official LibriVox AI-training policy was found. — [HF forum: policy for LibriVox audio](https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/policy-for-librivox-audio-data/46707); [LibriVox forum](https://forum.librivox.org/viewtopic.php?p=2304541); [LibriVox forum](https://forum.librivox.org/viewtopic.php?p=1940025); [LibriVox forum (TTS thread)](https://forum.librivox.org/viewtopic.php?p=1489575)
- **Common Voice:** downloads require agreeing not to attempt to identify speakers. — [HF Common Voice 16.0](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mozilla-foundation/common_voice_16_0)
- **Audiobook industry terminology** uses "cloning" for unauthorized replication where the human has not given permission. — [The Bookseller](https://thebookseller.com/news/industry-bodies-recommend-consistency-in-ai-audiobook-narration-terminology)
- **Model-provider measures:** Resemble AI watermarks every Chatterbox output (Perth). Kokoro's developer says its data excluded "custom voice clones". — [Chatterbox multilingual docs mirror](https://www.mintlify.com/yocxy2/chatterboxyocxy/models/chatterbox-multilingual); [Kokoro-82M card mirror](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M/blob/refs%2Fpr%2F44/README.md)
- **Google dataset authors** frame the Latin American corpora as free resources "unencumbered by a restrictive license" for low-resource TTS. I found no speaker-specific guidance for or against cloning one volunteer. — [Google Research publication page](https://research.google/pubs/pub49150/)

### Inferences
- Building "Nico" by fine-tuning on one Google volunteer's or one LibriVox reader's recordings is probably license-compliant if attribution and share-alike are honored. It is ethically weak by NAVA's "new use requires new consent" standard, and it is legally exposed in jurisdictions moving toward voice as protected biometric or personality data (Mexico in 2025–2026). Data per Google speaker is also too small for a good single-speaker clone, which removes the practical motive.
- **Ethically preferred options, in order:**
  1. An invented voice from a blend or design (Kokoro blend; Qwen3 VoiceDesign then freeze; Parler description then freeze).
  2. A multi-speaker model whose outputs do not reproduce any one identifiable person.
  3. A voice from someone who explicitly consents, such as the creator or a friend with a signed written release covering commercial, AI and recurring-character use. Thorsten-Voice is the precedent for a voluntary CC0 voice donation.
- **Disclosure:** for EU audiences, a fictional character with an invented voice is arguably not a "deepfake" of a real person, though that is untested. A voice cloned from a real dataset speaker could "resemble an existing person", which strengthens the case for disclosure. Adding a line such as "Voces generadas con IA" in descriptions is low-cost risk reduction. Platform-specific (YouTube/TikTok) AI-label rules were not researched here.
- For Kokoro, the "invented" status is strongest: blends of three stock voices whose training data the developer describes as permissive. A residual ethical nuance is that part of that data is synthetic output of closed commercial TTS voices, which ultimately trace back to licensed voice talent.

### Gaps
- Spain's personality-rights rules (e.g., protection of voice under Spanish law) and other Latin American national laws were not researched.
- The exact consent wording signed by the Google volunteers was not found.
- No official LibriVox or Mozilla position specifically on voice cloning of individual contributors was found.
- The current YouTube and TikTok synthetic-media labeling and monetization policies were not researched (likely covered by another researcher).
